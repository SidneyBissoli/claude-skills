# Ethics, Standards & Disclosure

This reference covers the ethical and transparency obligations attached to survey research:
duties to respondents, to clients, and to the public — and, most consequentially for any body
that *publishes* results (such as DataSenado), the **mandatory disclosure** that must accompany
released findings. These are obligations, not preferences; in review mode a clear breach is a
**defect** regardless of methodological tradition.

Source tags resolve in `source-map.md`. This file does not give legal advice on any specific
jurisdiction (e.g., Brazil's LGPD); it states the professional-ethics standards in the corpus
and flags where local law must be checked.

## Part I — The five fundamental principles (ICC/ESOMAR 2025)

*ICC/ESOMAR-CODE* (2025 revision) rests on five principles that underpin every article; legality
is the floor, and the Code holds standards "above and beyond legal requirements":

1. All research must be **legal, honest, transparent and truthful**.
2. All research must be conducted with **due care**.
3. Interactions must be **fair, respectful, and avoid harming the data subject**.
4. Researchers must **clearly communicate** how personal data will be collected and used, and
   **fully protect** all personal data against unauthorized access or use.
5. Researchers must **behave ethically** and not undermine public trust in research; they hold
   overall responsibility and oversight irrespective of method or technology.

The 2025 revision emphasizes accountability, transparency, and human oversight, and places an
added duty on the **client/commissioner** to ensure any contractors know and comply with the
Code.

## Part II — Duties to respondents (data subjects)

*ICC/ESOMAR-CODE* organizes these as articles on responsibilities to data subjects:

- **Duty of care** (Art. 1) — the overarching obligation toward respondents.
- **Children, young persons, and other vulnerable individuals** (Art. 2) — heightened
  protection; consent and special safeguards apply.
- **Data minimisation** (Art. 3) — collect only what the purpose requires (links to the
  "ask only what you need" discipline in `question-wording.md` and to keeping instruments
  succinct).
- **Primary data collection** (Art. 4) and **use of secondary data** (Art. 5).
- **Data protection and privacy** (Art. 6) — adequate security against unauthorized access,
  manipulation, and disclosure, including by third parties.

**Consent** is defined as a freely given, specific, and informed indication of agreement in
response to clear information (*ICC/ESOMAR-CODE*). **Confidentiality** of responses is core:
*ESOMAR/WAPOR-OP* stresses that respondents' rights extend through all stages, that
confidentiality is especially critical where improper disclosure could harm respondents, and
that adequate security must prevent unauthorized access or disclosure of personal data.

These duties also have a *measurement* payoff: credible confidentiality assurances reduce
social-desirability bias on sensitive items (cross-reference `question-wording.md` Part I-9).

## Part III — Duties to clients and to the profession

- **Fit for purpose** (Art. 7) — research must be designed and conducted to be fit for its
  stated purpose (the ethics-side statement of the skill's fitness-for-purpose doctrine).
- **Transparency, confidentiality and responsibility** (Art. 8) — toward clients.
- **Responsibilities to the research profession** — do nothing that undermines public
  confidence in research (principle 5 in article form).

## Part IV — Mandatory disclosure when publishing findings

This is the most directly actionable obligation for a publishing institution. *ICC/ESOMAR-CODE*
Article 9 governs **publishing findings**; *ESOMAR/WAPOR-OP* details what must accompany
published opinion-poll results so the public can interpret them. The published report is often
the public's only exposure to the poll, so disclosure is an ethical duty, not a courtesy.

**Items to be released *with* the published findings** (*ESOMAR/WAPOR-OP*):
- **Who conducted it** and **who commissioned/sponsored** it.
- **The universe effectively represented** — i.e., who was interviewed (e.g., all adults, or a
  defined group).
- **The achieved sample size** (number of completed interviews in the reported findings).
- **The dates of fieldwork.**
- **The sampling method** — and for quota/other **non-probability** samples, this must be
  stated (so results are not mistaken for probability-based estimates — links to
  `sampling-and-representativeness.md`).
- **The mode** by which the poll was conducted (face-to-face, telephone, internet panel, etc.).
- **Whether weighting was used** and the general demographic/behavioural weighting variables
  (proprietary algorithms and exact variables need not be exposed, but the general scheme must
  be described).
- **The actual wording of the relevant questions asked** — verbatim, to avoid ambiguity.

**Available on request** (a second, deeper tier — *ESOMAR/WAPOR-OP*): fuller methodological
detail including the **response rate**, and other particulars supporting interpretation.

**Honest reporting obligations.** Be transparent about sampling, weighting variables, question
wording, and timing; and **do not make claims about subgroups whose sample sizes are too small
for statistical reliability** (*ESOMAR/WAPOR-OP*). For a non-probability/self-selected base, do
not report a margin of error or imply representativeness (joins `sampling-and-representativeness.md`).

## Part V — Official-statistics standards (OMB)

For instruments produced under an official-statistics regime, *OMB-STD* expresses ethics as
binding standards across the survey lifecycle: define the target population and document the
design (Standard 1.2); evaluate the frame against the target population (2.1); pretest
components (1.4); design for adequate response and analyze/adjust/report nonresponse
(1.3, 3.2); and document and disseminate methodology so results are interpretable. The common
thread with the ESOMAR/WAPOR disclosure list is **transparency**: a result the user cannot
evaluate is, by these standards, incompletely delivered.

## Part VI — Where ethics meets design; review-mode map

Ethics is not a separate compliance layer bolted on at the end — several design choices *are*
ethics choices: data minimisation = not asking unnecessary (especially sensitive) items;
confidentiality = both a right and a bias-reducer; fitness for purpose = an ethical as well as
methodological duty; honest disclosure = the bridge to `sampling-and-representativeness.md`.

| Observation                                                          | Classify as |
|----------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------|
| Published results omit sponsor, sample size, dates, mode, or Q wording | Defect (disclosure breach) |
| Non-probability sample published without saying so                   | Defect      |
| Subgroup claims on samples too small for reliability                 | Defect      |
| No/!informed consent; unclear data-use communication                 | Defect      |
| Sensitive personal data collected beyond purpose (no minimisation)   | Defect      |
| Inadequate confidentiality/security for personal data                | Defect      |
| Children/vulnerable respondents without heightened safeguards        | Defect      |
| Disclosure provided in tiers (with-findings + on-request)            | Legitimate  |

A breach of these duties is not a "divergence" to be attributed to a tradition — it is a defect
to be fixed. Where the matter is legal rather than ethical (e.g., LGPD specifics), say so and
recommend qualified review rather than asserting a legal conclusion.
