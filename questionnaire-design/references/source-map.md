# Source Map — Traceability Ledger

This file is the **audit trail** for the `questionnaire-design` skill. Every substantive
recommendation in the other reference files must trace back to one or more entries here. The
rule (Doctrine #2) is simple: **if a claim is not supported by a source listed below — or by a
source you can name — do not assert it.**

All bibliographic details below were **verified against the files themselves**, not recalled
from memory. Where a file does not state a detail (e.g., a publication year), it is marked as
such rather than guessed.

## How to cite from this skill
- Refer to sources by their **short tag** (e.g., *CCSG-QD*, *AAPOR-BP*, *OECD-Trust*) in the
  reference files; the full reference lives here.
- When a recommendation rests on a single tradition, name that tradition (Doctrine #1).
- When the corpus is silent on a point, say so; do not extrapolate silently.

## 8. Data-integrity notes and relevance caveats

**Text-extraction files.** Five sources arrived as plain text saved with a `.pdf` extension
(not valid PDFs): `survey-methods-and-practices`, `designing-household-survey-samples`,
`handbook-european`, `household-individuals-foundations`, `task-force-comparative-surveys`.
Their prose is intact, but **tables and figures were linearised** during extraction, so any
table cell order may be scrambled. When a claim depends on a table or a numbered list in one of
these files, verify the structure before relying on it, or flag the uncertainty.
*(`measuring-trust` and `subjective-well-being` were originally in this list but have since been
replaced by verified original OECD PDFs — see §6; for SWB the verified source is the 2025 2nd
edition.)*

**Why these five are left as-is (deliberate decision).** Unlike the two OECD sources, these
five are **prose documents** — discursive methodological guidance, not item banks. The skill
draws **principles** from them (probability vs. non-probability inference, coverage error, mode
comparison, harmonisation, total survey error), not specific table values, and never reproduces
their tables. For the skill's intended scope — **questionnaire design and review, with sampling
only as inferential background** — the linearised prose is fully adequate, and no skill claim
depends on a scrambled table. Re-obtaining the originals would add only marginal robustness to
the occasional support table, not fix any real limitation. This was a deliberate scoping
decision, not an oversight. The only scenario that would justify re-obtaining the two
tabular-heavy manuals (`survey-methods-and-practices`, `designing-household-survey-samples`) is
an **expansion of scope into detailed sampling design** — which would be a separate, explicit
decision, not part of the current questionnaire-focused skill.

**Peripheral source.** `EUROSTAT-EBS` (short-term *business* statistics) addresses enterprise
surveys and statistical indices, not opinion/social questionnaire wording. Treat it as
out-of-core; cite only if a point about business-survey process or quality genuinely applies.
Do not draw item-wording guidance from it.

**Evidentiary weight (rough guide).** Official manuals and standards (STATCAN-SMP, STATCAN-QG,
OMB-STD, ESS-HB), the CCSG set, and AAPOR-BP carry the most weight for general practice. The
OECD construct guidelines are authoritative **for their specific constructs** (trust,
well-being). HARVARD-PSR and NG-2006 are practitioner/academic primers — corroborating, not
decisive. ESOMAR/WAPOR and ICC/ESOMAR are authoritative on **ethics, disclosure, and
opinion-poll conduct** specifically.
